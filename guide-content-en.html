SECTION 1

PRESENTATION OF THE AMERICAN DITEK INSTITUTE (ADI)

The American Ditek Institute (ADI) was created in 1992 and chartered in 1993 as the only English-speaking institute among the then six state universities in Cameroon. It started effectively in May 1993, in spite of the economic crisis facing the country in the early 1990s, the aura of currency devaluation, and the acute shortage of human, material and financial resources. The Institute boldly started with 768 students enrolled in three faculties. The Institute currently has seven faculties, one school and two colleges. The entire campus is networked and linked to the internet.

1.1. Mission

The mission of the American Ditek Institute (ADI) is to provide opportunities for quality education through teaching and research in an environment that is conducive to such pursuits and in ways that respond to market forces.

Conceived in the English-speaking tradition, the American Ditek Institute (ADI) seeks to foster the essence of that system while situating itself within the larger bilingual and multicultural context of Cameroon.

Its teaching and research programmes emphasize relevance, encourage tolerance and promote creative, critical and independent thinking.

SECTION 3

REGISTRATION PROCEDURES (NEW STUDENTS)

The American Ditek Institute (ADI) welcomes all new students to its campus for this academic year. As a new student, you are requested to follow the under-mentioned registration procedures very strictly to enable you to begin classes effectively. The registration procedures are as follows:

3.1. Step 1: Payment of registration fees

Fees are paid through the ADI Portal (“Fees and payments”) by MTN Mobile Money to +237 674 225 990. Each confirmed payment is issued with a numbered receipt that can be downloaded as a PDF from the portal. Pay only to this number: ADI staff never ask students to send money to another number. A payment counts only once it is confirmed.

The deadline for the payment of registration fees by new students will be announced by a notice of the Administrative Director and all students are called upon to respect the deadline. Those who do not fulfil their financial obligations by the stipulated deadline will lose their places.

3.2. Step 2: Verification and validation of receipts

The original receipt and two photocopies, together with the admission letter, should be presented for clearance to the Receipts Control Service of the Finance Office of ADI.

The Receipts Control Service will retain the original of the receipt while the two photocopies will be stamped and handed back to the student. The copy marked “STUDENT’S COPY” is to be kept by the student. This copy may be very helpful in case the student loses or misplaces his/her Institute ID card.

3.3. Step 3: Compulsory medical examination

The photocopy of the receipt marked “HEALTH CENTER COPY” should be attached to the test results and reports from ADI’s designated hospital or the American Ditek Institute (ADI) Health Centre and presented at the Health Centre of the Institute. The Health Centre will in turn issue an attestation which the student will present at his/her School for registration.

Test results and reports expected at the Health Centre at the time of registration of new students are:

  1. Chest X-ray;
  2. Blood sugar level;
  3. Weight and height;
  4. Blood pressure;
  5. Vision;
  6. Hearing;
  7. Tuberculosis;
  8. Urine analysis.

All these tests should be done either in an ADI-designated hospital or at the Health Centre of the American Ditek Institute (ADI). New students who opt to do the tests in the Institute Health Centre will pay a fee of 5,000 FCFA. The test results should be sealed, marked “CONFIDENTIAL” and addressed to the Head of the Health Centre, American Ditek Institute (ADI).

3.4. Step 4: Orientation

The schedule for orientation will be published by the Institute. All new students must undergo orientation before they commence lectures in their respective Departments. Students will be issued with tickets to show as proof that they have undergone orientation.

3.5. Step 5: Production of ID cards

The last photocopy of the receipt, marked “ID CARD CENTRE”, will be presented at the time and place that will be announced by the service in charge of producing ADI Institute ID cards.

Students are advised that if they have problems with their registration, they should seek assistance from the administration of the Institute.

SECTION 4

HEALTH INSURANCE BENEFITS

Each student of the American Ditek Institute (ADI) is required to undergo a medical examination at the beginning of each academic year before registration. The examination is obligatory.

The Institute provides health insurance for all registered students with the following benefits.

To benefit from the insurance policy

  • each student must have registered at the American Ditek Institute (ADI) Health Centre for the current academic year;
  • each student must have undergone the annual systematic medical examination;
  • all medical conditions must have been reported to the Institute Health Centre or referred by the Institute Health Centre to another medical centre.

Students are also informed that those who do not do the medical examination will not be allowed to register or sit for any examination organised by ADI.

The period of the medical examination goes alongside payment of fees and registration.

Students are to do the medical examination early so as to avoid congestion during the registration period. The procedure to be followed is specified in the notice to students on registration procedures.

SECTION 7

CODE OF CONDUCT OF ALL STUDENTS

As a corporate institution, the American Ditek Institute (ADI) accepts the responsibility of providing and ensuring the most congenial atmosphere for the academic, physical and moral development of its students. The following guidelines are set out for this purpose:

7.1.
The Institute expects students to conduct themselves decently and responsibly at all times, whether on or off campus.
7.2.
No student shall engage a fellow student, any member of the Institute or the public in physical scuffles.
7.3.
While it is not required that students appear in suits and ties or expensive clothes, they should be neat and presentable at all times. This applies especially to their appearance in class and on campus.
7.4.
All students should contribute to the cleanliness of the facilities put at their disposal. They should avoid littering and should make maximum use of the dustbins provided.
7.5.
No item of furniture should be moved out of the Common Room, Classrooms, campus or Library without permission.
7.6.
The full cost of lost or damaged property will be borne by the student responsible for such loss or damage.
7.7.
The junior staff of the Institute (messengers, drivers, security men, yardmen, etc.) are responsible officers of the Institute: students should not, on any account, insult or assault them. Any student aggrieved by any employee of the Institute shall make a report of such grievance to the Director of Students’ Affairs, who shall take the matter up with the appropriate quarters of the Institute.
7.8.
No student may be absent from class without prior authorization except for reasons of health, which should be justified. Students are to be punctual to class, show due respect to their teachers and maintain order and quiet in class.
7.9.
Students are advised not to smoke, as this is dangerous to their health. In any case, no smoking is allowed in the administrative buildings, restaurant, classrooms, lecture halls, laboratories and library.
7.10.
No liquor may be sold or consumed on the Institute campus before noon. Any student found drunk or misbehaving under the influence of alcohol, either on or off campus, shall be severely disciplined.
7.11.
The possession and/or consumption of any dangerous drug is prohibited. Any student found contravening this regulation shall be summarily dismissed from the Institute.
7.12.
Any student found stealing shall be severely disciplined in accordance with the Institute Regulations. Those found stealing outside the Institute premises may be handed over to the law enforcement officers.

THE IT UNIT

The Information Technology Unit (IT Unit) of the American Ditek Institute (ADI) is the nerve unit of computing power in the Institute, and serves also as a gateway to the external world. The Unit has an added advantage over similar centres around the country in that it simultaneously runs a fully equipped, ultra-modern Cyber Centre where Internet and office automation services are available for use in teaching, research and training purposes.

The IT Unit has been operational since 14 January 2021. The Unit was the first of its kind on our various campuses. It has a stable and reliable Internet connection to the outside world. Until recently, Internet connectivity to the Institute was ensured by CAMTEL through an optic fibre trunk with an uplink and downlink of 5 Mbps respectively.

SECTION 12

REGULATIONS GOVERNING THE AWARD OF DEGREES IN THE RESPECTIVE SCHOOLS

The present general regulations apply to all students of the Institute; however, students of other specialized programmes have additional provisions that are specific to their disciplines.

12.1. General provisions

12.1.1.
Instruction at the American Ditek Institute (ADI) shall be organized on the Semester Course Credit System; students shall be required to take such approved combinations of courses as the Senate, on the recommendation of the appropriate School Board, may from time to time determine.
12.1.2.
Courses shall be evaluated in terms of course credits.
12.1.3.
Each course shall be assigned the appropriate credits as recommended by the Ministry of Higher Education and the mentor institute, except as otherwise approved by the Senate on the recommendation of the School Board.
12.1.4.
A session at the American Ditek Institute (ADI) shall consist of two semesters of 12 weeks each.
12.1.5.
Each student shall be expected to register, at the beginning of the first semester, for all courses in the programme which he/she intends to pursue in an academic year. Such courses shall be entered on the prescribed registration forms and shall be lodged with the designated office in charge.
12.1.6.
Registration for a course shall not be deemed complete until a student has personally handed over all the completed registration forms to the designated office in charge, which shall duly acknowledge receipt of them.
12.1.7.
Registration for courses should be completed by the deadline prescribed by the Senate in the Institute Calendar. Students shall not write examinations in courses for which they are not duly registered.
12.1.8.
In accordance with the philosophy of the American Ditek Institute (ADI), which aims, among other things, at providing a broad-based and balanced education, the Institute requires that the student be knowledgeable in the sciences and humanities, as well as in Cameroon’s two languages. Each student shall consequently be required to pass a General Studies course (Civics and Ethics) as well as courses in the Use of English, Functional French, Sports and Information Technology skills.

12.2. Course terminology

12.2.1.
Cross-discipline courses: these are required for all students, regardless of their specialty. They include subjects such as French, English, civics and ethics.
12.2.2.
Fundamental courses: interdisciplinary courses that provide a solid background in the student’s chosen field.
12.2.3.
Professional courses: the core courses specific to the student’s field of study, designed to develop specialized knowledge and skills.
12.2.4.
Supervised industrial work experience scheme: a practical component where students undertake industrial attachments to gain hands-on experience and apply theoretical knowledge in real-world settings.
12.2.5.
Compulsory: a course specified for a degree/programme which a student must take and pass before graduation.
12.2.6.
Elective: a course which a student may choose in order to make up the required additional credits for the award of a degree.
12.2.7.
Prerequisite: a course whose knowledge is essential for another specified course.
12.2.8.
Concurrent: a specified course at the same level which must be taken during the same semester.
12.2.9.
Required: a course specified by a department which students must take. The minimum standard to be attained by students offering such a course is D, to earn credit.

A prerequisite shall be fulfilled by attaining a pass in the course. However, a student who fails the course but scores a minimum of D in it shall be deemed to have met the prerequisite requirement, but will not earn any credit for the course if it is compulsory for his/her programme.

SECTION 13

CHANGE OF SCHOOL OR DEPARTMENT OR SPECIALTY; PROCEDURE FOR ADDING OR DROPPING COURSES AND SUSPENSION OF STUDIES

13.1.
Students are not permitted to change the major for which admission was offered. Exceptionally, however, changes may be approved as prescribed in Articles 13.4 to 13.6 below.
13.2.
Students who wish to change any course for which they registered may do so upon completion of the form designed for the purpose during the prescribed period.
13.3.
The time limit within which students shall be allowed to drop or add courses on their list of registered courses shall be two weeks from the start of classes.
13.4.
Students who wish to change departments or specialty within the same School, or to transfer to another School, shall be required to fulfil the requirements for entry into the new Department, except as otherwise recommended by the School Board and approved by the Senate.
13.5.
A student authorized to transfer from one School, Department or specialty to another shall be credited with those courses in which a pass mark was obtained and which are within the curriculum of the new programme, as the Department or School may consider acceptable, but shall not carry forward failed grades obtained hitherto.
13.6.
Applications for change of programme shall be considered at the end of the academic year and shall only take effect from the beginning of the next academic year after approval by the Senate.
13.7.
A student who wishes to suspend studies must apply before the end of the semester(s) which the student wishes to suspend.
SECTION 14

SANCTIONS FOR VIOLATION OF REGISTRATION AND COURSE-RELATED REQUIREMENTS

14.1.
To qualify for suspension of studies, a student must be in academic good standing and also show proof of mitigating circumstances.
14.2.
A student who abandons his/her studies for four consecutive weeks without just cause shall be suspended. Such a student shall have to apply to the Senate for re-admission.
14.3.
A student who is absent for two consecutive sessions without just cause shall automatically forfeit his/her status as a student.
14.4.
Any student who registered for a course but failed to sit the corresponding examination for no just cause shall score zero in the course(s) for which he/she registered. Students who, for justifiable reasons such as ill health, do not sit for end-of-course examinations shall be given an “I” grade (incomplete). They will be allowed to re-sit the examination at the next opportunity.
14.5.
For a candidate to qualify to sit for an examination in a course, he/she must have attained at least seventy percent (70%) attendance at lectures, tutorials and practicals in that course.
14.6.
Students should be duly registered in only one School.
SECTION 15

PROGRAMMES OFFERED

15.1.
A programme of study in a School of the American Ditek Institute (ADI) shall lead to the award of a degree.
15.2.
Schools may offer the following: HND (Higher National Diploma), BTS (Brevet de Technicien Supérieur), Bachelor’s degrees and Master’s degrees.

15.3. HND/BTS

The training and duration of study for the HND/BTS programmes shall strictly adhere to the Ministry of Higher Education’s guidelines on training procedures and organization.

15.4. Bachelor’s degrees

The duration of a programme of study for the Bachelor’s degree shall respect the number of semesters, or such other number of semesters, as may be prescribed by the mentor universities.

The number of course credits required for a Bachelor’s degree shall be approved and issued by the mentor universities. However, it must not be less than the total programme credits, in addition to the general Institute requirements. Students must achieve a minimum Grade Point Average (GPA) of 2.00 on a 4.0 scale, as stipulated by the mentor institute.

The required number of credits to qualify for a major in a discipline shall match the acceptable credits for a single honors degree as stipulated by the mentor institute. Students enrolled in a double-major programme must earn the required credits in each major discipline in accordance with the mentor institute’s approvals. The required number of credits to qualify for a minor in a discipline shall be the number of credits approved by the mentor institute.

16.4.
To earn credit for a course the student must score a minimum of 50% (C) in that course if it is compulsory. Students may gain credit in the case of an elective/required course if they score 40% (D). In any event, the total number of D grades for which credit is earned may not exceed 10% of the total credits required for the programme.
16.5.
All courses shall be examined by continuous assessment through assignments and/or tests, and by examination. The grades obtained through such continuous assessment shall constitute 30%. However, a School may propose different proportions for particular courses, subject to the approval of the Senate and in compliance with the Ministry of Higher Education and the mentor institute. In any case, the proportions may not exceed a 50:50 ratio for continuous assessment and final examinations.
16.6.
If a student obtains a pass mark in a course after re-sitting the course once or any subsequent number of times, the final mark given shall be the average of the marks obtained in the different sittings if this average is higher than 50 percent, or 50 percent if the average is less than 50 percent. Only this average mark shall be used to calculate the student’s cumulative Grade Point Average (GPA). The fail mark shall no longer enter into the calculation of the cumulative GPA.
16.7.
For a student to remain in academic good standing, that is, not be asked to withdraw from the programme in his/her School, the following conditions must be satisfied:
16.8.
In each semester, students must register for courses totalling at least 30 credits (HND/BTS) or 24 credits (Bachelor), and not more than 32 credits if necessary. In exceptional cases, and with approval from the competent authority, a student in good standing may be allowed to register for courses totalling up to, but not exceeding, the required credits. 16.9.
A student who fails to obtain the required credits at the end of the first session (2 semesters), the second session (4 semesters), the third session (6 semesters) or the fourth session (8 semesters) may be asked to withdraw from the Institute.
16.10.
At the end of each semester, any student with a GPA of less than 2.0 shall be put on probation and shall be warned in writing. Any student with a GPA of less than 1.0 in two consecutive semesters shall be required to withdraw from the Institute.
16.11.
A student with a GPA of 3.25 or more at the end of any semester shall qualify to be placed on the Deputy Administrative Director’s list of excellence, while any student with a GPA of 3.5 and above shall qualify to be placed on the Administrative Director’s list of excellence, on condition that the student carried a full load and was not subject to any sanction.
16.12.
A student who has taken more than two semesters in excess of the approved period of study to complete a Bachelor’s degree programme shall not be eligible for an honors classification.
16.13.
Authorization, in accordance with the guidelines stipulated by competent regulatory bodies, must be obtained for any student seeking a pass degree who has spent more than the required semesters.
16.14.
A student who is duly registered at the Institute may seek deferment of studies on condition that such deferment does not exceed four semesters; after that, registration will lapse and the candidate wishing to resume will be expected to re-apply.
16.15.
All courses taught during each semester shall be examined at the end of the semester. Students shall earn the number of credits assigned to the course for which they have passed the examination, following the schedule provided in Article 16.1 above.
16.16.
Re-sit examinations in selected courses shall be offered to regularly registered students at the end of the examination sessions according to a schedule approved by the Senate.
16.17.
Examination results shall be released to students at the end of each examination session. Transcripts shall be issued to students at the end of each session.
16.18.
When it comes to issuing degree transcripts, the mentor institute overseeing the programme shall issue the transcripts, ensuring they adhere to the standards and procedures approved by the Ministry of Higher Education.

SECTION 18

EXAMINATION GUIDELINES

18.1.
All students who are duly registered for courses in a given semester are eligible to sit for examinations in those courses, except students in the following categories:
a.
A student who is absent from the Institute for more than six weeks without official permission;
b.
A student who fails to attend at least 70% of lectures, tutorials or practical sessions in any course.
18.2.
Students must acquaint themselves with the instructions governing examinations.
18.3.
Students must be punctual for their papers. Students arriving later than half an hour after the examination has started shall not be admitted into the examination hall.
18.4.
Students must bring their own ink, pen, ruler, eraser, pencil and any materials permitted by regulations to the examination hall. Students are warned that, unless otherwise indicated, lecture notes, cell phones, textbooks, jotters, bags, calculators, etc. are not allowed in the examination hall.
18.5.
To ensure order in the examination hall, seats will be arranged according to the number of groups taking examinations at each specific time. Candidates should not enter the hall earlier than 10 minutes before the commencement of each examination. They are advised to adhere strictly to the seating arrangements to avoid confusion. Under no circumstances should chairs arranged in examination halls be moved by any student.
18.6.
Once inside the examination hall, communication between students is strictly forbidden.
18.7.
Silence must be observed in the examination hall. The only permissible way to attract the attention of the invigilator is by raising a hand.
18.8.
Smoking in the examination hall is strictly forbidden.
18.9.
Use of scrap paper is strictly prohibited. All rough work must be completed in the answer books, neatly crossed out, and submitted along with the answer booklet.
18.10.
Students must use their matriculation number for each examination.
18.11.
Before handing in their scripts at the end of the examination, students must ensure that they have correctly inserted their matriculation numbers and the numbers of the questions answered in the appropriate places.
18.12.
Distinctive marks of any kind on the answer booklets are strictly forbidden. Only blue or black ink is permitted for writing on examination answer booklets.
18.13.
Students are prohibited from leaving the examination hall during the first 30 minutes and the last 50 minutes of any examination.
18.14.
Students must remain seated until invigilators have finished collecting all answer booklets.
18.15.
Students are required to sign against their matriculation numbers on the attendance sheet.
SECTION 19

EXAMINATION MISCONDUCT AND DISCIPLINARY SANCTIONS

In accordance with Decree No. 93/027 of 19 January 1993, which establishes common regulations for all universities, particularly Articles 61 to 66, it is an offence for any student, staff member or any person to impersonate a student in any Institute examination. Any student or staff member found guilty under this regulation shall be subject to disciplinary sanctions. This does not preclude prosecution by law enforcement authorities.

19.1.
Any student or staff member who attempts to unlawfully obtain or provide pre-knowledge of an examination question, or to influence the marking of scripts or the awarding of marks by the examiner, shall be subject to disciplinary sanctions.
19.2.
Any student suspected of such offences is required to submit a written statement to the invigilator immediately after the examination. Failure to provide this statement shall be considered an admission of guilt.
19.3.
Any student suspected of cheating, receiving assistance, or assisting another student shall be required to submit a written statement to the invigilator immediately after the examination. Failure to provide this statement shall be regarded as an admission of guilt.
19.4.
The Dean/Administrative Director shall promptly set up a disciplinary panel made up of:
MemberRole
The Dean / Administrative DirectorChairperson
Vice-Dean / Deputy Administrative DirectorVice-Chairperson
1 member designated by the DeanMember
1 member designated by the Vice-ChancellorMember
1 representative of the School Students’ AssociationMember

Where an investigation report, with a recommendation of sanctions, is submitted to the Administrative Director, and the Administrative Director determines that a student has breached any of these regulations, the student shall be deemed to have committed an examination offence. The Administrative Director may then:

  1. Give public notice of the offence;
  2. Issue a written warning to the student, which may include partial or total suspension of all Institute aid or assistance;
  3. Suspend the student from any Institute examination for the current session and suspend all aid or assistance;
  4. Recommend to the Board of Directors that the student be temporarily suspended from the Institute for one or two sessions;
  5. Recommend to the Board of Directors that the student be dismissed from the Institute and barred from attending any other institution in the country.
19.5.
Where the Administrative Director has reason to believe that the nature of any question or the content of any paper may have been known before the examination to any persons other than the examiner(s), the Board of Examiners, or any authorized Institute official, he/she may order the suspension of the examination, the cancellation of the paper, or the setting of a new paper, and shall report the matter to the Senate. The Administrative Director shall take any disciplinary measures against the staff or student as provided by the regulations in force.
19.6.
Any student or member of the academic staff may lodge a petition with the Administrative Director through the General Secretariat of ADI if they believe that the examination has been improperly conducted. The Administrative Director shall investigate the complaint and report the results to the Senate, which shall take appropriate action, including withholding results or depriving the award of a degree or diploma, etc.

19.7. Absence from examination

19.7.1.
Students must present themselves for all Institute examinations for which they have registered under these regulations. Students who fail to do so, except in cases of accidents or illness duly justified by a medical report from the Institute’s Chief Medical Officer, or other exceptional circumstances, shall be deemed to have failed that examination. Misreading the timetable or similar lapses will not be accepted as satisfactory explanations for absence.
19.7.2.
A student who falls ill during an examination shall report in writing to the Administrative Director of the Institute.
19.7.3.
A student who is absent from an examination due to illness, confirmed by medical evidence from the Head of Institute Health Services, shall be allowed to take the regular examination at the next opportunity without repeating the course if they so choose. Approval for make-up examinations shall be granted by the Senate based on the recommendation of the School Board.
